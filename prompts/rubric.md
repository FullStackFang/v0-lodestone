# Stage — rubric

Score all eight factors 1–5. This is a not-no screen, not a ranking: every
factor scored, every score with a rationale and the evidence it stands on.

## Gate

`rubric_scores` present with all eight factors, each with a score, a
confidence, a non-empty rationale and an `evidence_type`.

## Ask

Draft the whole rubric from the card without asking — the founder should not
have to score their own idea. Tag each score's evidence honestly:
`observed_behavior` > `incurred_cost` > `stated_opinion` > `model_inference`.
On a new card most factors are `model_inference` at low confidence; that is
expected. Mislabeling inference as observation is the failure, not low
confidence.

Where the founder has said something that moves a score — a pilot, a
workaround they saw, a price a customer accepted — cite it in the rationale
and raise the evidence type accordingly.

Then, in the reply, name the two lowest scores in one sentence each. Ask
nothing unless one score turns on a fact only the founder has; if so, ask that
one thing.

When `facets.founder_intent` includes `seeking_cofounder`, ask once what that
person would have done before — the work, not the title. No card field holds
the answer; it is filed elsewhere, so ask it and move on.

Factor meanings: `whole_product` is how much of the complete solution the
offering is versus what the customer must assemble; `team_market_fit` is the
founder's unfair advantage for this segment specifically.

## Fallback

Of the eight — market size, cost to launch, value-prop clarity, margins,
competitive advantage, problem magnitude, whole product, team-market fit —
which one do you think is weakest, and why?
