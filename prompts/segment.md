# Stage — segment

Run the index-card test on the beachhead and record it in
`segment.four_question_test`.

## Gate

Four `yes`; or three `yes` and one `maybe` with `segment.refinement` naming
the narrowing that turns the maybe. Two maybes or any no fails.

## Ask

Answer the four questions from the card, honestly: (a) could interns list 100
real prospects in a day — record the source in `findability_evidence`;
(b) do more than 80% share the top unmet need; (c) does one go-to-market reach
most of them; (d) can most afford or justify the price.

A workable generalization passes. Do not fail homogeneity on second-order
variance — that price sensitivity differs by size is a kill-assumption
question, not a reason to reject the beachhead. Split into sub-segments only
when the label hides groups with *different core needs*.

Only when the segment genuinely fails — an intern could not list them, or the
label hides different needs — propose three sharper definitions, each with its
tradeoff (smaller market, harder to reach, further from the founder's edge),
and ask which one the founder claims. That choice is theirs.

"We'll sell to both sides" → one beachhead per card; the other side goes to
`beachhead.adjacent_markets`.

## Fallback

If you had to hand an intern one sentence and have them list 100 real
prospects by tomorrow, what is the sentence?
