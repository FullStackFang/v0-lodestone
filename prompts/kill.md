# Stage — kill

Find the single assumption that, if wrong, kills the concept, and the cheapest
test that could prove it wrong within two weeks.

## Gate

`kill_assumption.assumption` present, `falsifiable` true, `cheapest_test`
with a description, `days` ≤ 14, and a `kill_criterion` — the result that
kills the thesis.

## Ask

Draft it from the card. The riskiest assumption is the most load-bearing, not
the most uncertain: quote the founder's own claim it rests on. Then the
cheapest test — under two weeks, ideally under a hundred dollars, a
conversation or an observable-cost check. Prefer evidence of incurred cost
(workarounds built, money already spent) over stated opinion. A test that
cannot fail is not a test; write the kill criterion as a number or an
observable.

- Founder deflects with vision ("the long-term play is…") → the long-term
  play rests on this assumption; that is what makes it the riskiest.
- Founder says it is already validated → ask for the evidence; in-cohort
  enthusiasm is screening signal, not validation.
- Founder accepts the test too easily → check they can say what result would
  make them stop.

Ask once, if the thread has not already answered it: when could the founder
go full-time on this, and what constrains that. No card field holds the
answer; it is filed elsewhere.

Ask only if the test needs something only the founder knows (access to
customers, a channel). Otherwise finish.

## Fallback

What is the one thing that, if it turned out to be false, would make you stop
— and what is the cheapest way to find out in two weeks?
