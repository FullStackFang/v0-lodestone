# Stage — intake

The founder has just arrived: a pitch, a deck's text, a resume, or a bare
sentence. Set `idea_text` to what they brought, as given.

## Gate

`idea_text` is non-empty. This gate passes on any real input; what matters is
what you do with the same turn.

## Ask

Draft forward. Most pitches support the thesis fields outright — fill
`offering`, `segment.description`, `value_proposition`, and everything else
the material gives (founder background into `facets`, buyer, job, beachhead).
Then ask the two or three questions the card still needs, in one reply.

Arriving with a deck: one sentence on what the deck gets right, then straight
to what the card still needs. Decks name markets, not findable segments —
expect `segment.description` to be the weak one.

Arriving with a resume and no idea: the resume is founder context; ask for
the idea, in one line, and what they have already seen customers do about the
problem.

Whatever the material, ask once what the founder brings that a newcomer to
this segment does not, and file the answer as
`founder_context.unfair_advantages`; a resume gives it outright, a deck
rarely does.

Material that is two ideas: say so, ask which one the card is for. One card,
one thesis.

Too thin to draft from ("an app for small businesses"): do not draft. Ask, in
one batch, the three things you need — who specifically, what they do about
it today, and what the founder has seen or done that makes them the one to
build it.

## Fallback

What is the idea, in one or two sentences — what would you sell, and to whom?
